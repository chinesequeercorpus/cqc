Corpus

CQC Release 1.0 contains about 10.5 hours of Mandarin speech from 54 speakers, drawn from public Bilibili and YouTube videos, transcribed and aligned at word and phone levels.

Units

  • Speaker: an individual whose speech is represented in CQC, identified only by a CQC speaker ID.
  • Source recording: the original public video.
  • Session: the speech of one CQC speaker taken from one source recording. Genre is coded per session.
  • Segment: a stretch of a session that is transcribed and aligned.

A speaker may contribute several sessions. Report speakers, sessions, and segments separately in analyses.

Selection

Each speaker passed every step below. To protect speakers, we do not publish the names of channels or videos.

  1. Candidate discoverySearches on Bilibili and YouTube with Chinese and English terms for gender and sexual minority identities.
  2. Self-description screeningKept only speakers who explicitly described themselves this way in their own videos or public profile.
  3. LanguageMandarin speech, including regional varieties; other Chinese languages such as Cantonese excluded.
  4. Recording qualityAt most two people speaking; no persistent background music, canned laughter, heavy noise, or heavy overlap.
  5. Speaker inclusionAt least five minutes of usable speech per speaker; only the target speaker’s speech is included.

Annotation

Gender identity, trans status, sex characteristics, and sexual orientation are different things, so each is recorded in its own field. Each speaker’s own words are kept as they said them, because Chinese community terms do not map neatly onto English categories.

Not stated does not mean absent. A field is marked not stated whenever the speaker did not say it explicitly. We do not infer missing information from appearance, voice, biography, or any other source. For example, “not stated” under sex characteristics does not mean “not intersex,” and a speaker is coded cis only if they said so.

Why voice-training content is flagged

Some recordings discuss or demonstrate voice training. Demonstration speech may differ from a speaker’s everyday speech, so these sessions are flagged. The flag is a property of the recording, not of the speaker’s identity, and should not be treated as a proxy for gender identity.

Processing

  1. Audio preparationConverted to 16 kHz mono WAV.
  2. Speaker diarizationTwo-speaker recordings separated with pyannote.audio; target-speaker segments checked by hand.
  3. TranscriptionAutomatic transcription with Whisper; all 5,183 segments reviewed by trained annotators, and 64.2% accepted without change.
  4. NormalizationSimplified characters; punctuation removed.
  5. Forced alignmentWord and phone boundaries with the Montreal Forced Aligner, producing Praat TextGrids.

Further quality figures are given in the datasheet. Processing scripts are available on request.

Other figures

  • Age was stated by five speakers (21–74 years). For the others, age is not stated and was not estimated.
  • 50 speakers (92.6%) were recorded indoors.

Limitations

  • Representation. Cis women, bisexual, non-binary, and older speakers are few.
  • Platforms. Bilibili and YouTube users are not representative of Mandarin speakers in general.
  • Publicness. CQC includes only people willing to speak publicly online about their identity.
  • Genre. Most speech is semi-structured; scripted and spontaneous speech are limited.
  • Geography. Speakers’ regional backgrounds are uneven and often not stated.
  • Self-presentation. Public videos involve deliberate self-presentation for an audience.
  • Labels. Local identity labels do not always correspond to English categories; analytic categories simplify them.
  • Time. Online speech and identity vocabulary change; recordings reflect when they were made.
  • Recording conditions. Microphones, rooms, and compression vary, as in any collection of online media.

Releases

Material may be removed between releases at speakers’ request. Always cite the release you used.
