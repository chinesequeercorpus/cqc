Chinese Queer Corpus

CQC is a research corpus of Mandarin Chinese speech from speakers who publicly identify as members of gender and sexual minority communities. It supports research on how gender, sexuality, and speech relate in Mandarin.

Why this corpus

Research on gender, sexuality, and the voice has been based mostly on English and other Western languages. Large Mandarin speech corpora exist, but they record gender as male or female, if at all, and say nothing about sexuality.

CQC fills part of this gap. It records how each speaker describes themselves, in their own words, alongside separate fields for gender identity, trans status, sex characteristics, and sexual orientation. Speech is transcribed and time-aligned so that researchers can study pronunciation, pitch, and speaking style.

CQC is not a tool for guessing who is gay, trans, or queer. Our analyses show wide variation within every group and large overlap between groups. Using the corpus to infer anyone’s identity from their voice is prohibited.

Questions

Can I listen to the recordings here?

No. To protect speakers, no audio is published on this website. Researchers can apply for access to the annotated data under a data use agreement.

Who is included?

Adults who have described themselves as members of a gender or sexual minority community in their own public videos on Bilibili or YouTube. We record only what speakers have said about themselves; we never infer identity from voice or appearance, and we do not use what others have said about them.

I think my video is in CQC. What can I do?

You can ask us to remove it at any time, without giving a reason. See how to request removal or the 中文说明.

I am not a linguist. Can I use CQC?

Researchers in sociology, gender studies, anthropology, communication, and related fields are welcome to apply, as long as the project is non-commercial research. The public documentation on this site is open to everyone.
