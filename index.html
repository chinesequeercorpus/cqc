Chinese Queer Corpus

A research corpus of Mandarin speech, sex, gender, and sexuality

Chinese Queer Corpus (CQC) is a speech corpus of speakers from sex, gender, and sexuality minority communities. The current release contains about 10.5 hours word- and phone-level aligned Mandarin speech data extracted from public videos retained from Bilibili & YouTube.

sex, gender, sexuality, and speech

CQC documents variation in queer speech. Our analyses show wide variation within every group and large overlap between some groups. Using the corpus to infer anyone’s identity from their voice is prohibited.

Why CQC

Research on language, sex, gender, and sexuality, and the voice has produced a substantial body of work, but most of it concerns English and a small number of other languages. Many large Mandarin speech corpora provide limited information about sex, gender, and sexuality, and generally do not include detailed self-described gender amd sexuality metadata. CQC provides a resource for studying these relationships in Mandarin Chinese.

CQC is designed for questions such as:

  • How does phonetic variation in Mandarin relate to sex, gender,and sexuality, and how much does it vary within groups?
  • How do speakers use linguistic resources to construct identity?
  • How do locally meaningful Chinese queer identity labels relate to linguistic practice?

What CQC does not claim

CQC does not assume that:

  • queer speakers share a single way of speaking;
  • gender identity determines how someone speaks;
  • sexual orientation can be identified from the voice;
  • acoustic variation correspond directly to queer identities;
  • its speakers represent Mandarin-speaking queer communities as a whole.

CQC is not a corpus for defining a “queer voice.”

Questions

What is a speech corpus?

A speech corpus is a collection of recorded speech, organized and annotated for research. In CQC, speech is transcribed, and each word and sound is time-stamped so that researchers can explore pronunciation, pitch, and timing.

Why is audio not publicly available?

Public availability does not by itself make redistribution appropriate or unrestricted. Collecting recordings into a labelled research corpus can make speakers easier to locate and analyze systematically.

Who is included?

Speakers who described themselves as members of the queer community in their own public videos on Bilibili or YouTube.

I think my video is in CQC. What can I do?

You can ask us to remove it at any time, without giving a reason. See how to request removal or the 中文说明.

I am not a linguist. Can I use CQC?

Yes. Researchers in any research fields can apply. See For Researchers.
