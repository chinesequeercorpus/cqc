For Researchers

CQC is open to non-commercial research in linguistics and in related fields. You do not need a background in phonetics to use it. This page shows what the corpus supports, what it cannot show, and what to check before you apply.

What you can study

The examples below are possible directions, not a guarantee that the current corpus can fully answer each question. Whether it fits depends on your research design and on the limitations described on the Corpus page.

FieldExample questionsKeep in mind
Phonetics and sociophoneticsHow do speakers differ in pitch, vowels, voice quality, or speaking rate, within and across groups?A group difference does not mean every individual follows the same pattern.
Sociolinguistics and discourseHow do speakers present themselves, take positions, and use community labels such as 酷儿, 无性恋, or 跨性别?Speech features cannot be interpreted apart from their context.
Gender and sexuality studiesHow do speakers talk about identity, community, and personal experience in public videos?The speakers do not represent all members of sexual and gender minority communities.
Sociology and anthropologyHow do public narratives reflect community practices and self-presentation online?A public video is not a full record of a person’s life or experience.
Speech technologyHow well do speech recognition and other systems work across speakers and recording conditions?Results must be reported in aggregate; classifying identity is prohibited.

Different projects need different data. Some can start from transcripts and metadata; others need the time-aligned speech. Say in your application which data you need and why.

What CQC cannot show

  • It cannot tell you anyone’s gender identity or sexual orientation from their voice.
  • It cannot show that a speech feature belongs only to one identity group.
  • Its statistics cannot be generalized to all Mandarin speakers or all queer communities.
  • Self-descriptions in public videos cannot reconstruct a speaker’s full identity, history, or relationships.
  • It may not be used for identity classification, speaker identification, or voice cloning.

What the corpus contains

Approved users receive transcripts, Praat TextGrids, speaker and session metadata, timestamps with download scripts, audio files, and source information (channel names, account handles, video titles). See the corpus documentation and the datasheet for details.

Researcher checklist

  • Read the corpus documentation and the datasheet.
  • Read the Data Use Agreement, especially the prohibited uses.
  • Obtain ethics approval from your institution if it is required.
  • Decide whether your project really needs speaker-level identity information.
  • Treat identity categories as social, not biological or acoustic, categories.
  • Do not try to identify, find, or contact any speaker.
  • Report sample sizes, imbalance, and limitations.
  • Refer to speakers only by CQC speaker IDs, and cite the CQC release you used.

Related corpora

CQC is part of a small but growing set of resources on gender- and sexual-minority speech. The list below is assorted rather than complete, and each resource has its own design, access rules, and limitations. Check the original publication before you use or cite it.

General speech datasets with some gender-diverse labels

These datasets offer gender options beyond the binary, but very few speakers use them. An audit of six English speech datasets found that 0–1.4% of speakers identified outside the gender binary (Sheppard et al.).

Collections of Chinese queer materials

ResourceContentsAccess
The Leiden Chinese Queer CollectionPrimary sources and scholarship on the Chinese queer experience across the Chinese-speaking world and its diasporas: literary works, films, monographs, journal publications, and magazines. Not a speech corpus.Held by Leiden University Libraries; see the project page.

We know of no other public corpus of Mandarin or other Chinese speech from gender- and sexual-minority speakers. Acoustic studies such as Geng and Gu (2022) on gay and heterosexual men and Huang et al. (2024) on trans women are, as far as we know, not released as corpora.
