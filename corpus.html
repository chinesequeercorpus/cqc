The Corpus

CQC contains about 10.5 hours of Mandarin speech from 54 speakers, drawn from publicly available recordings on Bilibili and YouTube, transcribed and aligned at word and phone levels.

Contents: Sources · Annotation · Processing · Statistics · Limitations

Sources and inclusion

Speakers were found on Bilibili and YouTube using Chinese and English search terms for gender and sexual minority identities. A speaker was included only if they explicitly described themselves this way in their own videos or public profile.

  • Mandarin speech, including regional varieties; other Chinese languages such as Cantonese were excluded.
  • At most two people speaking; only the target speaker’s speech is included.
  • Recordings with persistent background music, canned laughter, heavy noise, or heavy overlap were excluded.
  • At least five minutes of usable speech per speaker.

To protect speakers, we do not publish the names of channels or videos.

Annotation scheme

Identity is recorded in separate fields, because gender identity, trans status, sex characteristics, and sexual orientation are different things. Each speaker’s own words are kept as they said them, because Chinese community terms do not map neatly onto English categories. Anything a speaker did not state publicly is recorded as not stated.

Processing

  1. Audio preparationConverted to 16 kHz mono WAV.
  2. Speaker diarizationTwo-speaker recordings separated with pyannote.audio; target-speaker segments checked by hand.
  3. TranscriptionAutomatic transcription with Whisper, then reviewed by three trained annotators; 5,183 segments were reviewed.
  4. NormalizationSimplified characters, punctuation removed; mixed English and digits handled as documented in the user guide.
  5. Forced alignmentWord and phone boundaries with the Montreal Forced Aligner, producing Praat TextGrids.

Limitations

  • The corpus reflects who speaks publicly online: cis women, bisexual, non-binary, and older speakers are few.
  • Most speech is semi-structured; scripted and spontaneous speech are limited.
  • Recording conditions vary, as in any collection of online media.
  • Categories are only as complete as what speakers chose to say about themselves.
