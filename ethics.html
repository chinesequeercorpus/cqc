Ethics & Speakers

The speakers in CQC chose to speak publicly. Collecting their voices into a labelled research corpus still creates new risks, so we limit what we record, what we share, and who can use it.

Principles

  • Self-identification only. We record only what speakers said about themselves, in their own content. We never infer identity from voice or appearance, and never use what others said about them.
  • Speakers’ own words. Self-descriptions are kept as stated, so that Chinese community terms are not replaced by outside categories.
  • Identity is not a sound. CQC documents variation; it is not a dataset for classifying people.
  • Minimal sharing. No audio, channel names, or images are published. Annotated data go only to approved researchers.
  • Speakers can leave. Material is removed on request, and users must delete it too.

Safeguards

  • Before each release, every source is checked again. Material whose video has been deleted or made private is removed.
  • Distributed metadata exclude channel names, account handles, video titles, and visual material.
  • Every user signs the Data Use Agreement, which prohibits identity inference, re-identification, contacting speakers, and voice cloning.
  • We keep a record of every approved user so that removal notices reach everyone who holds the data.
