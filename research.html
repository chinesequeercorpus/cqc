For Researchers

CQC is open to non-commercial research in linguistics and in neighbouring fields. This page shows what the corpus supports, what it does not, and what to check before you apply.

What you can study

Phonetics and sociophonetics

Pitch, vowel production, voice quality, segmental variation, speech rate, and their variation within and across speakers.

Sociolinguistics and discourse

Style, stance, self-presentation, and identity work in talk; how speakers use community labels such as 同志, 拉拉, or 跨性别.

Gender and sexuality studies, anthropology, sociology

How queer and trans speakers in Mandarin-language online media talk about identity, community, and experience, based on transcripts and metadata.

Speech technology

Evaluation of how speech systems perform across speakers, reported in aggregate only. Training or evaluating systems that classify identity is prohibited.

What the corpus contains

Approved users receive transcripts, Praat TextGrids, speaker and session metadata, and source timestamps with download scripts. See the corpus overview and the datasheet for details.

Researcher checklist

  • Read the corpus documentation and the datasheet.
  • Read the Data Use Agreement, especially the prohibited uses.
  • Obtain ethics approval from your institution if it is required.
  • Decide whether your project really needs speaker-level identity variables.
  • Treat identity categories as social, not biological or acoustic, categories.
  • Do not try to identify, find, or contact any speaker.
  • Report sample sizes, imbalance, and limitations.
  • Refer to speakers only by CQC speaker IDs, and cite the CQC release you used.

How to get access

  1. Read the documentationCorpus page, datasheet, and agreement.
  2. Apply by emailSend the application template on the Data Access page.
  3. ReviewWe review applications on a rolling basis and may ask follow-up questions.
  4. SignThe PI and an institutional official sign the agreement.
  5. DownloadYou receive a personal download link.
